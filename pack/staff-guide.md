# The Staff Guide

For everyone who will work next to the AI employee. Print it, pin it, and
hand it to new hires. It answers the four questions staff actually ask.

## "How do I ask it for something?"

Ask like you are briefing a capable new hire:

- **The task** — "Draft a reply to this enquiry."
- **The background** — paste the actual message or document. Give it the
  source, not your summary of the source.
- **What done looks like** — "Two short paragraphs, friendly, offer the
  two open slots."

One task per request. Vague requests get vague work, from humans and AI
alike.

## "It got something wrong. Now what?"

Two fixes, in this order:

1. **Fix the output** so today's work is right.
2. **Fix the source** — the fact, price, or policy lives in its memory
   folder or on its one-pager. Correct it there, dated, in plain words:
   "We never offer discounts on the phone."

A correction that only happens in chat is gone tomorrow. A correction
written into memory is permanent. If it gets the same thing wrong twice,
that is a one-pager problem: tell the lane owner.

## "Where does it keep what it knows?"

In the **memory folder** — files the business owns:

- `memory/MEMORY.md` — the facts: hours, prices, policies, decisions.
- One playbook per lane — how that lane does its job.
- One one-pager per lane — what the lane owns and what it may never do.

If it is not written in the folder, the AI employee does not know it.
Decisions made in meetings go into memory the same day.

## "What will it never do?"

- Send, publish, or pay for anything without a human approval first.
- Contact anyone on the do-not-contact (scrub) list.
- Invent a price, a policy, or an availability. It will say it does not
  know and draft a question for a human instead.
- Argue with a correction. The human owner's version is the final one.
- Work outside its lane. A lane that does everything is a lane nobody
  owns.

## "Something looks wrong. What do I do?"

Pull the kill switch (it is named in the governance sheet — anyone may
pull it), then tell the lane owner what you saw. Restarting the lane is
the owner's decision, and only the owner's.
